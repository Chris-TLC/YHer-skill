# Anticipated Reviewer Objections and Response Matrix

This is an internal red-team artifact. It separates objections already answerable
from objections that require new evidence. It does not authorize inserting an
unobserved result into the manuscript.

| ID | Anticipated objection | Why the objection is credible | Current manuscript response | Evidence required before insertion | Red-team disposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R01 | The simulator and inference model are circular. | The matched generator samples from production likelihoods. | Matched and misspecified generators are separated; H4 is limited to one perturbation family. | Broader perturbation families or external response data would be needed for a stronger robustness claim. | Keep as a central limitation; do not answer with H4 alone. |
| R02 | There are no human outcome data. | Programmatic and provider outputs cannot establish student behavior or learning. | The title, abstract, discussion, and ethics statement limit claims to simulation and internal behavior. | A separately approved human study would be required for behavioral or efficacy claims. | Accept the limitation; venue fit must tolerate simulation-based system audits. |
| R03 | The M/P/C/U labels may not be valid constructs. | They are engineered operational states, not externally established traits. | The manuscript consistently calls them model-defined diagnostic states and avoids ontological claims. | Independent construct work and human response evidence would be needed to upgrade the labels. | Do not defend the labels as psychological truth. |
| R04 | H1 sounds positive even though the absolute criterion failed. | The A-minus-B contrast was positive, but Arm A reached only 12.9% correct convergence. | H1 is described as partially supported, with the 50% threshold and no-repeat sensitivity beside the primary contrast. | None; the frozen result is sufficient if reported without spin. | Preserve the negative absolute result in abstract and conclusion. |
| R05 | H2's fixed-quota pattern is cherry-picked. | C exceeded A, but A also exceeded B beyond the no-harm margin. | The three arms and both contrasts are co-reported, and H2 is described as not supported. | None. | Reject any wording that frames H2 as confirmed harm prevention. |
| R06 | Sparse pools and repeat replenishment drive H1. | The broad support permits family reuse; no-repeat support is small and much less favorable. | Both estimands are reported, including 1/200 versus 0/200 on four-target common support. | A larger family-distinct catalog would be required to resolve the sensitivity. | Treat as a major generalizability constraint. |
| R07 | Arm-C failures make the three-arm comparison selective. | Four targets cannot instantiate the fixed prerequisite schedule, producing 1,600 structural failures. | The lifecycle count, affected-arm logic, and support restrictions are disclosed. | A pre-declared intention-to-treat alternative or expanded pools would be needed for a different estimand. | Do not impute zero or favorable Arm-C outcomes. |
| R08 | The terminal-versus-convergence mechanism is post-hoc. | Selector composition was examined after H1-H4 and was not randomized. | The manuscript labels every mechanism statement post-hoc and calls it a selector-stopping mismatch interpretation. | A frozen stopping-rule replay or factorial selector-by-stopper study is required for mechanism confirmation. | Keep the finding descriptive and proposal-generating. |
| R09 | The headline percentages mix target supports. | A prior narrative paired a full-set terminal rate with an eligible-set convergence rate. | The journal table reports the 27-target pair and 23-target pair separately, with exact denominators. | A dedicated target-set hash and support-aware binder should gate later automatic insertion. | Block any cross-support percentage pair. |
| R10 | Persona-v2 controlled prompts measure answer-state shifts, not target-policy compliance or learner resemblance. | Explicit behavior constraints can manufacture answer differences, while exact target mappings exist for only 6/100 rows. | Confirmatory reporting is limited to paired deficit-control answer-state composition and shifts; target-option compliance is sparse descriptive evidence only, and blind robustness is separate. | Better mappings plus human response data would be needed for target-policy or resemblance claims. | Never convert prompted shifts into compliance, authenticity, or human similarity. |
| R11 | Provider calls inflate the Persona-v2 sample. | Each persona is observed repeatedly across providers, prompt conditions, deficit-control arms, items, and repeats. | The independent unit is 50 persona clusters; every listed factor is repeated within cluster, and the bootstrap resamples clusters. | The final analysis must retain cluster identifiers and rectangular-support denominators. | Reject call-count sample sizes. |
| R12 | Automated adjudicators are not independent semantic truth. | Cross-model judges may share biases and can agree while being wrong; one model family may also be unavailable. | The protocol reports pairwise agreement only when two distinct model families complete; otherwise it discloses the missing judge and retains single-family coding as exploratory. | Human blinded coding would be needed for an external semantic reference. | No realism score, consensus truth label, or fabricated pairwise statistic. |
| R13 | Text-only prompts omit known hard cases. | Diagrams, apparatus, equations, and multi-step open responses are major diagnostic weaknesses. | Text-only is stated in Methods and limitations, with no multimodal claim. | A separately frozen multimodal protocol and validated assets would be required. | Treat as a scope boundary, not a minor implementation detail. |
| R14 | The mapping collapse makes misconception compliance unmeasurable. | Exact consensus exists for only 6 of 100 calibration rows. | Target-misconception hit rate was removed before provider observation; six rows are sparse descriptive only. | Better item/failure/option mappings reviewed independently would be needed for a confirmatory target-hit metric. | Do not restore the metric after observing providers. |
| R15 | The prescription analysis may manufacture coverage. | The 13-node/68-chunk parent library overlaps the H1-H4 targets at only two strata, eight chunks, and three physical sources. | P2 is illustrative and reports that exact support; structural failure, missed available supply, unused budget, and null unobtainable dose remain separate. | Broader role-compatible supply and externally validated dose definitions would be required for a remediation claim. | Preserve Arm C's exact 1/2 failed-node denominator; never impute coverage or extrapolate to full chemistry. |
| R16 | Extensive AI assistance weakens authorship accountability. | AI generated the initial full prose and supported code, analysis, figures, revision, and review. | Methods and Declarations state those roles, exclude AI authorship, and make critical human review a submission prerequisite. | Tool/model names, versions, the selected journal's formula, and a completed human critical-review record are required before submission. | Disclose specifically; do not imply a universal publisher policy or completed human review. |
| R17 | The intervals look like target- or learner-population inference. | The bootstrap resamples 50 simulator replicates within each fixed target; targets and learners were not sampled. | The abstract, Methods, Results, and limitation 5 define intervals as Monte Carlo uncertainty conditional on the fixed catalog. | A target-sampling design or human sample would be needed for population inference. | Reject any population-general CI interpretation or use of 1,150 as a learner count. |
| R18 | The paper is internal QA rather than a scientific contribution. | It audits one implementation, offers no new selector, and has no external outcome data. | The contribution is framed as a repository-frozen selector-stopper compatibility audit with preserved negative results, same-support reporting, and reusable failure distinctions. | A second system, stopping-rule intervention, or external study would strengthen transferability. | Venue fit must value transparent methods/system audits; do not manufacture novelty. |
| R19 | Provider and API drift undermine reproducibility. | Commercial model identities, serving stacks, and stochastic outputs can change after collection. | Requested and returned model identities, lifecycle status, prompt bytes, and dates are recorded; temporal drift remains a limitation. | Archived allowable outputs or a later frozen replication would be needed for stronger temporal reproducibility. | Report the observation window and model identities; never imply timeless provider rankings. |
| R20 | P2's 160,000 profile rows create false precision. | The rows are fixed product-form integration terms formed from two 50-replicate target margins, not joint learners or independent observations. | Methods give the 1/16 truth-cell and 1/2,500 within-cell weights and deny learner sample size or population-inferential CIs. | Real joint profiles and an empirical prevalence model would be needed for population inference. | Label any bootstrap interval as simulator Monte Carlo variability only and keep product terms out of `n`. |

## Required Final Checks

   persona-clustered, hash-bound analysis artifact.
2. Verify that the P2 slot retains every requested arm or its explicit structural
   failure without imputation, including the exact 1/2 failed-node denominator and
   null `unobtainable_supply_minutes` field.
3. Re-run the full manuscript blacklist, citation, absolute-path, and rendering gates.
4. Verify every paired percentage has identical support, filter, weighting, and
   denominator semantics.
5. Reconcile the AI declaration and data-availability language with the selected
   journal's current policies.
6. Verify that no interval is described as target-, learner-, school-, or
   system-population inference.
7. Verify that P2's 160,000 product-form rows never appear as a learner sample size or
   as independent observations.
