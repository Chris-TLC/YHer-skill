YHer — An Evidence-Bound Diagnostic Learning System

A high-school chemistry tutor whose every conclusion is traceable: every diagnosis to the exact answer behind it, every solution to a verified source, every recommendation to a signed evidence trail.

The problem

In the gaokao system, the scarcest resource is not content — it is judgment. Good teachers can always answer the follow-up question: “which of my answers made you say that?” Most students never get that.

AI tutoring in 2026 is generation-first: a model produces a diagnosis or a hint directly from its weights. Even when it is right, it cannot show its work — its statements are not linked, by construction, to any particular student response or source document.

The design bet

Not “make the model smarter.” Instead: make every answer a student sees traceable to something that cannot be invented.

  • Standard solutions are shown only if they passed verification against official answer keys.
  • The item bank is recovered from original exam Word documents — never model-generated.
  • The browser fails closed: answers, rubrics, and item IDs never reach it before a response is locked in.
  • The AI layer only selects and phrases verified content. It cannot invent chemistry facts.

What was measured

  • The data funnel: 6,083 raw slices → 1,202 serviceable items, a 19.8% end-to-end yield — the price of requiring every answer to be verifiable and every figure renderable.
  • The generation boundary: in five blind rounds (¥13.87 total), judges distinguished AI-generated questions from real ones 87% of the time from scratch and 65% overall after style transfer (60% under fair conditions). Generated items therefore never enter the diagnostic path.
  • The architecture audit: 4 keep / 10 modify / 5 replace / 2 downgrade across 21 engine components. The hardest finding: every hand-set constant in the engine lacked calibration evidence. Three fixes are already implemented and tested.
  • Governance incidents as evidence: an agent-loop runaway and a printed-answer leak became the design rules that followed.

What it does not claim

No real-student trials. No retention or learning-effect evidence. Every conclusion is simulation-, audit-, or engineer-derived — and the report says so in every section, in the same sentence as the claim. That honesty is the product’s central design principle, applied to its own documentation.
