YHer
makes student thinking visible.

Education does not lack content. What is scarce is the judgment to notice where a student's thinking breaks down and know what to ask next.

YHer is an open attempt to build that judgment into a Shanghai high-school chemistry system: every diagnosis points to evidence, and every next step can be tested. I cleaned and released the chemistry dataset on Hugging Face.

01 / Why this exists

A good teacher does more than explain the answer.

In a one-to-one lesson, the valuable moment is often not the explanation. It is the teacher noticing the exact step where a student's thinking went off course.

Was the prerequisite missing? Was the concept understood but the reasoning chain unstable? Was it a careless slip, or simply not enough evidence yet? A good teacher asks the next question that separates those possibilities, then chooses the right explanation and checks again.

Most learning products make content abundant. They do not make that judgment available. YHer asks a practical question: can AI show what it noticed in a student's work, explain why it matters, and test its judgment with the next question?

The goal is not to make AI sound like a teacher. It is to make every judgment answer a simple question: "What in this student's work supports it?"

02 / The learning loop

Every conclusion points back to evidence.

01 / SOURCE

Start with real work

Public Shanghai examination papers and the answer sources that accompany them, not invented training examples.

02 / STRUCTURE

Keep the document intact

Recover text, formulas, tables, figures, and answer blocks as linked objects.

03 / DIAGNOSE

Ask the next useful question

Score on the server, update a four-state belief, and ask what will reduce uncertainty most.

04 / TEACH

Give a grounded next step

Show a source-checked solution when available, then route the student to a traceable video or practice resource.

05 / VERIFY

Check transfer, not recall

Use practice and unseen question families to test whether the reasoning actually moved.

06 / REMEMBER

Leave a replayable trace

Append events, update the profile, and keep the next session accountable to this one.

The loop is intentionally narrow: one knowledge point, one session, one evidence trail. A smaller claim is easier to verify.

03 / The data foundation

The dataset is a chain of decisions, not a pile of text.

The item bank comes from original Word documents. The pipeline preserves text, formulas, tables, figures, and source records, then removes items that cannot support a reliable diagnosis.

Each drop in the count records a decision: if we could not check the answer, render the question clearly, or trace it to a source, we left it out.

The code is MIT-licensed. The chemistry dataset is open on Hugging Face under CC BY-NC 4.0, with field semantics and rejection rules included.

04 / The architecture

A diagnosis engine, not a chatbot with a score.

Evidence in Papers + keys

Structured items, a knowledge graph, and traceable resources.

↓
Session engine Score, update belief, choose the next question

Four possible states, uncertainty reduction, and unseen verification families keep the path inspectable.

↓
Evidence out A useful next move

Grounded explanation, resource recommendation, verification, and a replayable profile.

A language model can help organize the response; each claim still has to lead back to a source and a student's work.

01 / mastery.py

Belief: what might be happening?

Tracks four possibilities: mastered, prerequisite gap, unstable reasoning, or simply not enough evidence yet.

Algorithm: Bayesian updating + time decay (FSRS).

02 / selector.py

Choice: which question will tell us most?

Chooses the next item by how much uncertainty it is expected to remove, while keeping seen items and verification families out.

Algorithm: expected information gain.

03 / planner.py

Time: what fits this session?

Turns 30, 60, 120, and 180-minute sessions into explicit work budgets, then stops when the evidence runs out.

Rule: bounded work beats false certainty.

04 / recommender.py

Action: what should come next?

Ranks traceable video resources after diagnosis, with track, time-budget, and seen-segment controls.

Rule: recommendation follows judgment.

05 / memory.py

Continuity: what carries forward?

Retrieves useful past events for the next explanation without letting memory rewrite the diagnosis.

Model: replay the trace, do not invent it.

05 / A necessary boundary

AI can organize context. It cannot yet be the one-to-one teacher.

This project is an engineering attempt, not a substitute for a teacher. It can make a judgment easier to inspect, but it still sees only a partial record of a student's thinking.

The engineering limit

A structured trace is still a partial view.

The system can connect an answer to a source, a cautious hypothesis, and a follow-up test. It still depends on a narrow set of deterministically gradable questions and carefully recovered figures; it does not reproduce a teacher's full conversational read.

The model limit

A long prompt is not long-term attention.

Current LLMs can retrieve supplied context, but they do not maintain a teacher's selective attention across weeks. Dense figures, formulas, and earlier misunderstandings can still be misread or lost.

The human question

AI should extend teaching, not impersonate it.

AI can make careful questions and useful resources more available. The teacher still holds the long view, the relationship, and responsibility for what happens next.

My position is simple: make careful judgment more available, keep uncertainty visible, and never confuse a convincing answer with understanding.

06 / Open by design

Leave the work in a form others can build on.

I published more than a demo. The repository includes the code, data schemas, rejection rules, audit history, and the report behind every number.

Open work lets other people begin with what this project has already learned. They can inspect the assumptions, reuse the pipeline, and push the research further.
